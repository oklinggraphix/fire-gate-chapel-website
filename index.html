Responsibility Is Inescapable

Rev. Dr. Godwin Ahiahornu

“No one can take responsibility for your life. You must learn to take responsibility for yourself.”

Responsibility is an unavoidable part of life. Whether at home, in school, at work, in ministry, or in our relationships, we all have responsibilities that come with the choices we make.

If you refuse to take responsibility for your life, you may eventually become someone else's working tool. Taking responsibility means understanding that your decisions, actions, attitudes, and choices have consequences—and being willing to accept those consequences.

Take Responsibility for Your Life

If you don't take responsibility for your life, someone else may begin to make decisions for you.

A responsible person keeps his or her promises, completes tasks on time, keeps commitments, and does not constantly make excuses. When something should take one day to complete, unnecessarily stretching it into several days only creates delay.

Procrastination can become a serious obstacle to progress. Sometimes, the next level of your life requires you to take the first step before you see God's intervention.

There are moments when you have to make an attempt before God steps in. Faith does not mean doing nothing. You must be willing to move, act, and do what is within your power while trusting God for what is beyond your power.

Responsibility Is Part of Every Area of Life

Responsibility is important in every part of life:

  • At home: Be dependable and contribute to the well-being of your family.
  • At school: Take your studies seriously and complete your assignments.
  • At work: Be punctual, committed, honest, and dependable.
  • In relationships: Keep your word, communicate honestly, and accept responsibility when you are wrong.
  • In your personal life: Make wise decisions and take ownership of the direction your life is taking.

When we take responsibility, we demonstrate maturity. We earn people's trust, become better individuals, and position ourselves for growth and achievement.

Learn to Admit Your Mistakes

One of the clearest signs of responsibility is the ability to admit when you are wrong.

Responsible people do not spend their lives blaming others. They acknowledge their mistakes, accept correction, learn from their experiences, and make the necessary adjustments.

Accepting correction does not make you weak. It shows that you are willing to grow.

You cannot change yesterday, but you can learn from it.

Responsible people don't live in the past. They learn from the past and move forward with greater wisdom.

Every Choice Has a Consequence

Every action or decision you make carries consequences. Those consequences may be positive or negative depending on the choices you make.

This is why you must think carefully before taking significant steps in life.

Whatever step you are taking, sit down and think twice, because every decision comes with consequences.

Do not make permanent decisions based on temporary emotions.

Before you act, ask yourself:

  • What could be the result of this decision?
  • Who could be affected by my choice?
  • Is this decision taking me closer to where I want to go?
  • Will I be proud of this decision later?
  • What lesson can I learn from this situation?

Wisdom is not simply knowing what to do; it is also understanding the consequences of what you choose to do.

Sometimes People Will Use You—Learn From It

In life, there will be moments when people take advantage of you or use your kindness for their own benefit.

When that happens, don't allow the experience to destroy you. Take note. Learn from it. Become wiser.

Being used once should teach you something. If you refuse to learn from the experience, you may continue to find yourself in the same situation.

Responsibility also means taking ownership of the lessons life teaches you.

You cannot control everything people do to you, but you can control how you respond and what you learn from the experience.

Responsibility Builds Trust

Responsible people are reliable.

When people know that you will do what you said you would do, they learn to trust you. Trust is built through consistency, honesty, commitment, and accountability.

If you say you will do something, do it.

If you make a mistake, admit it.

If you receive correction, learn from it.

If you make a commitment, honour it.

These simple qualities can distinguish a responsible person from an irresponsible one.

Embrace Personal Responsibility

You must learn to embrace personal responsibility because it can unlock your true potential.

Many people struggle to accept the responsibilities that come with their choices. But as long as we are alive, making decisions and navigating life, we will encounter lessons, challenges, successes, and failures.

Life will teach you lessons, and you must be prepared to accept them and learn from them.

Sometimes, where God is taking you requires preparation. The challenges you encounter may become part of the training that prepares you for your next level.

The lesson may be difficult, but the growth that comes from it can be valuable.

Don't Blame Life—Learn From Life

Life will not always go according to your plans.

You will make mistakes. People will disappoint you. Opportunities will be missed. Some decisions will produce results you didn't expect.

But responsibility says:

“What can I learn from this?”

Instead of constantly asking, “Who is to blame?”, learn to ask, “What is my responsibility in this situation, and what can I do differently?” That mindset can change your life.

Responsibility Creates a Better Tomorrow

No one can take responsibility for your life.

Your parents cannot live your life for you. Your friends cannot make every decision for you. Your pastor can guide you, but you must ultimately make your own choices. Other people can support you, but you must take responsibility for your journey.

Your future is influenced by the decisions you make today.

Therefore:

Be responsible.

Stay honest.

Keep your word.

Accept correction.

Learn from your mistakes.

Think before you act.

Do your best in everything you do.

Responsibility today can create a better tomorrow.

Final Thought

Responsibility is not a punishment; it is a pathway to maturity, growth, trust, leadership, and lasting success.

You cannot control everything that happens in life, but you can take responsibility for your choices, your attitude, your actions, and your response to life's circumstances.

Wherever life takes you, be ready to learn.

Whatever you do, do it with integrity.

Whatever mistake you make, learn from it.

Whatever responsibility has been placed in your hands, handle it faithfully.

No one can take responsibility for your life. Embrace personal responsibility, because it can unlock your true potential and lead you toward lasting success.

Be You.

Responsibility today makes a better tomorrow.

Calling & Purpose

Rev. Dr. Godwin Ahiahornu

Purpose Comes First

God created every person for a purpose.

When you discover your purpose, you stop focusing only on the distance to your destination and begin to appreciate the benefits of where God is taking you. You begin to understand that the journey has meaning because there is a divine intention behind your life.

Purpose answers the question:

“Why was I created?”

Your purpose is the overall intention God has for your life. It is the bigger picture of what God wants your life to accomplish.

“For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them.”

— Ephesians 2:10

You were not created accidentally. Your life has divine significance.

The Purpose Every Believer Shares

Although people's individual assignments may differ, there is a fundamental purpose that every believer shares. We were created:

  • To know God
  • To love God
  • To glorify God
  • To reflect His character
  • To serve His purposes
  • To advance His Kingdom
  • To bear fruit that brings glory to His name

“By this My Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit; so you will be My disciples.”

— John 15:8

Your profession may change. Your location may change. Your responsibilities may change. But the ultimate purpose of your life remains connected to God and His glory.

Purpose Is Bigger Than Your Job

One of the greatest mistakes people make is confusing their occupation with their purpose.

Your career is what you do. Your purpose is why you do it.

You may be a teacher, doctor, businessperson, pastor, administrator, farmer, engineer, designer, parent, or entrepreneur. These are different expressions of life, but your ultimate purpose is to glorify God through the life He has given you.

Many people have learned a trade, developed a skill, or entered a profession through which they can fulfill part of their God-given purpose.

Your work can become a platform for your purpose.

Purpose and Calling Are Closely Connected

Purpose and calling are not exactly the same thing, but they are closely related.

Purpose answers: “Why was I created?”

Calling answers: “What specific assignment has God given me?”

Purpose is the bigger picture.

Calling is the specific assignment through which that purpose is expressed.

Purpose is the “why”; calling is the “what.”

God may give two people the same ultimate purpose—to glorify Him—but give them completely different callings.

That is why you should never measure your life by somebody else's assignment.

What Is Calling?

Calling is the specific task, role, responsibility, or assignment that God entrusts to an individual.

It is the particular way God chooses to use your life to accomplish His purposes.

Some are called to preach.

Some are called to teach.

Some are called to lead businesses.

Some are called to raise families that honour God.

Some are called to missions.

Some are called to leadership.

Some are called to give generously.

Some are called to serve quietly behind the scenes.

Others may never stand on a platform, but their faithfulness may influence generations.

The calling may be different, but the purpose remains connected to God.

Biblical Examples of Calling

The Bible gives us many examples of people whose assignments were different.

Moses — Called to Lead

God called Moses to deliver Israel from Egypt and lead His people toward the Promised Land.

“Come now, therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt.”

— Exodus 3:10

David — Called to Be King

David was anointed and called to lead Israel as king.

His calling was different from Moses' calling, but both were part of God's larger purpose.

Paul — Called to the Gentiles

Paul received a specific apostolic assignment. God said:

“He is a chosen vessel of Mine to bear My name before Gentiles, kings, and the children of Israel.”

— Acts 9:15

Paul's calling was specific. He knew the people and assignment God had entrusted to him.

Apollos — Called to Teach

Apollos was gifted in communicating and defending the message of Christ. Different calling. Different expression. Same Kingdom.

This teaches us an important principle:

God does not call everyone to do the same thing, but He calls everyone to live for His glory.

Calling Is an Assignment From God

Think about entering a large business facility where the manager has a trusted employee whom he loves and sends to represent him.

When that employee enters another office, he does not go representing himself. He goes representing the manager who sent him.

That is a picture of calling.

When God calls you, He is entrusting you with an assignment that represents His purposes.

You are not simply doing what you like. You are responding to the One who sent you.

“You did not choose Me, but I chose you and appointed you that you should go and bear fruit.”

— John 15:16

Calling involves both being chosen and being sent.

There Is Grace for Your Calling

God does not call you without making provision for the assignment.

When God gives an assignment, He also gives grace, wisdom, strength, gifts, opportunities, and people necessary to accomplish it.

This is why you should not constantly compare your calling with someone else's.

The grace on another person's assignment may not be the grace God has given you.

What looks easy for another person may be difficult for you because it is not your assignment.

And what others struggle to do may come naturally to you because God's grace is upon your calling.

Giving Can Also Be a Calling

Giving is another area where God may place a special grace and assignment on a person's life.

Some people have a remarkable capacity to give. They can sacrifice, support God's work, help people, and release resources without allowing possessions to control them.

Paul speaks about those who give with generosity as part of the different expressions of grace within the body of Christ.

“He who gives, with liberality.”

— Romans 12:8

Such people may be called to use their resources to advance God's purposes.

They may give their last, trust God for provision, and continue to support the work of the Kingdom.

Their calling may not look like preaching, but their contribution can make preaching possible.

The Bible Is a Manual and a Menu

The Bible is both a manual and a menu.

It is a manual because it teaches us how to live.

It is a menu because it contains a variety of spiritual truths, principles, examples, warnings, promises, instructions, and revelations that must be properly presented to God's people.

Therefore, those who carry the Word must learn to become like a skilled chef.

A chef does not simply put every ingredient on a plate. He knows what to prepare, how to prepare it, and what is appropriate for the people he is serving.

Likewise, those called to teach and preach God's Word must prayerfully prepare a balanced spiritual diet.

“Study to show yourself approved to God, a worker who does not need to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth.”

— 2 Timothy 2:15

The goal is not simply to know Scripture, but to faithfully communicate God's truth in a way that feeds, corrects, strengthens, and equips His people.

Do Not Abandon Your Calling

When you reject your calling, you can find yourself struggling in an assignment God never gave you.

You may try to fit yourself into somebody else's ministry, career, platform, or lifestyle because it appears successful.

But another person's calling is not necessarily your calling.

If you continually step into another person's assignment, you may delay your own development and miss opportunities God prepared specifically for you.

The question is not: “What is everyone else doing?”

The question is: “Lord, what have You called me to do?”

There Is Peace in God's Assignment

When you discover your calling, accept it, and position yourself where God wants you to be, there is a deep sense of alignment.

It does not mean there will be no difficulties.

Calling does not eliminate sacrifice.

Calling does not eliminate opposition.

Calling does not mean everything will always be easy.

But there is a particular peace that comes from knowing:

“I am where God wants me to be, doing what God has called me to do.”

Some people are called to places of great sacrifice so that God may be glorified through their obedience.

The sacrifice may be great, but so is the eternal significance of obedience.

Stop Comparing Your Journey

One of the greatest dangers in discovering purpose is comparison.

You may look at another person's success and assume you should be doing exactly what they are doing.

But God is not asking you to reproduce somebody else's journey.

David could not fight Goliath wearing Saul's armour.

He had to fight with what God had prepared him to use.

Your calling requires you to discover your own identity, develop your own gifts, and follow God's direction for your life.

“But by the grace of God I am what I am.”

— 1 Corinthians 15:10

There is freedom in knowing that you do not have to become somebody else.

Purpose Gives Meaning to the Journey

When you understand purpose, you stop measuring your life only by how far you have to go.

You begin to see the value of the journey. You understand that some seasons are preparation.

Some seasons are sacrifice.

Some seasons are training.

Some seasons are hidden.

Some seasons are opportunities to serve.

And some seasons are platforms where God allows your gifts and calling to become visible.

The destination matters, but the journey is also part of God's preparation.

Discover Your Purpose. Discern Your Calling. Fulfil Your Assignment.

Your purpose is bigger than your profession.

Your calling is bigger than your title.

Your assignment is bigger than your position.

God created you intentionally, and He has given your life significance.

Therefore, do not spend your life trying to become someone else.

Discover why God created you.

Discern what He has called you to do.

Develop the gifts He has placed in you.

Submit your plans to Him.

And faithfully fulfil the assignment He has entrusted to you.

“You did not choose Me, but I chose you and appointed you that you should go and bear fruit.”

— John 15:16

You were created for a purpose.

You were called for an assignment.

And when purpose and calling come into alignment, your life becomes a powerful instrument in the hands of God.

Find your purpose. Answer your calling. Glorify God with your life.
